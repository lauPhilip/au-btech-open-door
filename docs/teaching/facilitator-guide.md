# Facilitator guide

This guide is for the teacher who runs Open Door in a class. It takes you from an empty room to a finished debrief, minute by minute, for both the 45 and the 90 minute version, and it works for both Nordknit scenarios, "Black Friday" and "New bank details". You do not need to prepare anything besides reading this page once and checking that the projector shows the big screen.

## What you are teaching

Open Door is a first lesson in threat modelling with the CyPro method. The students are not asked to know anything about security. They walk around a small fictional company, talk to its people, look at the things on its desks and write what could go wrong on sticky notes. When the incident begins they follow clues, decide together what the CEO should do, and finally compare their notes with the answer key. The learning goals are the ones printed in the scenario: find the data a company depends on, see where it leaves the company's control, name what could go wrong and who could cause it, and compare your own view with what really happened.

## Before the class

Open the teacher page on the computer you will teach from and press "Create session". Pick the scenario and the length, and write down the four-letter code. The session belongs to the browser that created it, so use the same browser until the class is over, and do not clear its data. Press "Open the big screen" and drag that window to the projector. If there is no projector, press "Copy the join link" and send it in the course chat, or read the short address aloud from the teacher page.

Check the network once with your own phone. Open the join link, pick a face and a team, and watch yourself appear on the big screen. Students need nothing but a phone or laptop with a browser, and the game works on eduroam and on guest wifi. If the wifi is poor, the paper backup kit described in the issue tracker is the fallback.

## The 45 minute version

The class opens with five minutes for joining. Tell the students to scan the code on the big screen or to open the link, to type a first name or nickname, to pick a face and to sit at a team table. Five teams exist, named after door colours, with up to five students each. Walk around while they join. When the teacher page says nobody is without a team, press "Start the story".

The story takes about three minutes. Karin, the CEO, speaks on the big screen and on every phone, and the students tap through. Do not talk over her. Press "Start walking" when the last student has reached the end.

The walk lasts twelve minutes and is the heart of the lesson. Students walk their characters around the office, tap people to ask questions, tap things to look closer and write sticky notes. Say only one thing before you start: that a note should say what could go wrong, who could cause it and how likely it is. Then stay quiet. Watch the team overview on your page. A team that has not moved for three minutes is flagged, and one tap on the hint button sends a short nudge to that team's phones. The plus-five-minutes button gives the class more time if the notes are still flowing, and the pause button stops every clock if you need to speak to everyone.

When the timer ends, or earlier if every team has stopped writing, press "Start the day". Notes are shared with each student's team automatically, so the team now sees one board. The game clock begins at five to eight, the alert says that something has stopped working, and messages arrive from the characters. The day lasts fifteen minutes in the short version. Students follow clues in the rooms they visit, merge duplicate notes and agree on what is happening. Your job is to be visible but silent. If a team is stuck, send a hint rather than an answer.

When the day ends, press "Start the vote". Each team decides what Karin should do right now, and the majority at the table counts. The teacher page tells you how many students have voted. Three minutes are enough. Press "Show the result" when every team has decided, or when the three minutes are over.

The last seven minutes are the debrief. The big screen shows which team saw which problem coming, the CyPro names for what they found and one question at a time from the scenario. Read the question aloud, give the teams thirty seconds to talk, and call on two or three. End on the second question, which is the one that connects the game to the next lecture. Before you close the session, press the buttons that download each team's notes as a CyPro table and the class summary, then close the session so every name and note is deleted.

## The 90 minute version

The long version uses the same steps with more time where thinking happens. Allow five minutes for joining and five for the story, since the students will read more slowly and ask each other questions. The walk lasts twenty-five minutes, which is enough for every team to visit all nine rooms and to talk to every person twice. The day lasts thirty minutes, and the vote five. The debrief gets twenty minutes, so you can use all four prompts, and you should pause on the table that shows who saw what, because the differences between the teams are the real content.

If you have a little time to spare, use the walk to run a short check halfway through. Ask the teams how many of the things worth protecting they have found, which the students can see in the sidebar, and which room they have not visited yet. Send a hint to teams that are missing something.

## Running the debrief well

Start with the result and not with the correct answer. Ask a team that scored well what made them look in a particular room, because the answer is usually a habit that others can copy. Then ask a team that missed a threat why it was easy to miss. In "Black Friday" the shared admin password is almost always missed, because a note under a keyboard looks like a person's problem and not a company's. In "New bank details" the single approver is almost always missed, because the person doing the work has done it correctly for years. These misses are the lesson, and the prompts in the scenario are written to bring them out.

Keep the CyPro names for last. Students learn faster when they have already described the threat in their own words and you then show the name for it. The debrief screen puts the names beside the table for exactly this reason.

## If something goes wrong

If a student's phone loses the connection, nothing is lost. The game keeps their name, team, place and notes, and the phone reconnects on its own. If a student closes the browser, they can join again with the same link and will be put back where they were. If a student joined the wrong team before the walk, use the move-student control on your page. If the whole class is finished early, press "End now and show summary". If you lose the teacher page, open the teacher address in the same browser; from another browser the session will not accept you as teacher, and you will need to start a new one.

## After the class

Download the class summary and, if you want to give feedback, each team's table. Nothing about the students is kept after you close the session, and the room deletes everything six hours after it was created in any case. For the next lecture, the scenario names the topic that follows from this one, and it is printed under the last debrief question.

## Testing this guide

The issue asks for a test by a teacher who was not involved in building the game. Before using this guide in a course, hand it to a colleague together with a laptop and ask them to run a short session with three friends, without any help from you. Write down every moment where they hesitated or asked a question, and change the guide or the game until nobody has to ask.
