Open Door

A walk-through game where student teams explore a real-feeling company, spot what could go wrong, and live through the day it does.

Students walk around a top-down office with their teammates. When they talk to someone or look closely at something, an illustrated close-up opens. They leave sticky notes on risks. Then the scenario plays out, and the class compares what each team saw coming. Behind the play sits the CyPro method from the Digital Business Development toolbox: assets, system model, threats and likelihood — named only at the end.

Anatomy of a sticky note

ON: LABEL PRINTER Anyone on the guest Wi-Fi could stop it on Black Friday. It would: stop the work Who: a visitor or driver Likely? Seen in companies like ours — Laura
On: a thing or a room→ asset and component in the system model
What could go wrong→ the threat, in the student’s own words
It would leak / change / stop→ confidentiality, integrity, availability
Who might cause it→ threat actor
How likely→ CyPro’s own four likelihood levels, in plain words

Likelihood without jargon

CyPro already offers a simple four-step likelihood scale based on experience. The game uses exactly those steps, so the notes drop straight into the CyPro threat table.

1Could happen in theory
2Heard of it elsewhere
3Seen in companies like ours
4It has happened here
Each team member writes notes privately first, then the team sees them together and merges duplicates. This gives quieter players a voice and mirrors CyPro’s advice to vote before discussing.

Eight rules every screen follows

Each rule comes from a well-known UX law or guideline, turned into something concrete for this game, so designers, developers and reviewers can check the same things.

JAKOB’S LAW · FAMILIARITYFeels like tools they already useChat looks like a messaging app, notes look like sticky notes, the office looks like an office.
HICK’S LAW · FEW CHOICESAt most three choices at a timeA close-up offers two or three actions. The note asks one question per row.
MILLER · CHUNKINGSmall lists, always visibleSeven rooms on the checklist. Up to five clues per scenario.
FITTS’S LAW · EASY TARGETSBig, forgiving click areasWhole rooms are clickable. Buttons at least 44 px. Walking never needs precision.
DOHERTY · FAST FEEDBACKEvery action answers within 0.4 sMoving, sticking a note and teammates’ moves appear instantly, even on eduroam.
PROGRESSIVE DISCLOSUREDepth only when asked forA “look closer” link shows logs and STRIDE terms for those who want them.
PEAK-END RULEA clear peak, a good endingThe day is the peak. Every team ends on “what you saw coming”, never only on mistakes.
READABILITY · PLAIN ENGLISHShort sentences, everyday wordsFlesch Reading Ease 60+ and sentences under 20 words, checked automatically on every scenario text.

Accessible by default

WCAG 2.2 level AA is the baseline, in line with what public-sector sites in the EU must meet. Text keeps a 4.5:1 contrast. Colour never carries meaning alone: risk levels also have numbers, and teammates have initials. Everything works with the keyboard — arrow keys to walk, Tab to reach rooms and buttons — and screen readers get a room list as an alternative to the map. Animations respect the “reduce motion” setting.
